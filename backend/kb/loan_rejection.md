# Credit Underwriting and Loan Rejection Policies

## Understanding Rejection Due to High FOIR Debt Ratio
Fixed Obligation to Income Ratio (FOIR) measures the proportion of monthly net income committed to debt servicing. Under lending partner underwriting benchmarks, personal loan applications with a total FOIR exceeding 50% are automatically declined to prevent customer over-indebtedness. To qualify upon reapplication, borrowers must bring their cumulative debt commitments below 45% of net monthly income. Foreclosing smaller consumer durable loans or pre-paying active credit card balances is the recommended corrective pathway.

## Low Credit Score and Bureau Reporting Remediation
Credit bureau scores (such as CIBIL or Experian) below 700 are a primary ground for loan decline. A low score typically stems from past overdue payments, loan defaults, or high credit card balance utilization exceeding 30% of total sanctioned limits. To rebuild credit standing, borrowers should maintain uninterrupted on-time repayments across all credit lines for at least 3 consecutive billing cycles and verify credit reports for potential reporting inaccuracies or fraudulent inquiries.

## The 90-Day Mandatory Cooling-Off Period
Following an automated underwriting decline, a mandatory 90-day cooling-off interval applies before a new loan application can be considered. Submitting multiple loan applications across financial institutions within this window triggers hard credit inquiries, which can further depress the credit score by 10 to 30 points. Borrowers are enrolled in a synthetic 90-day recovery milestone tracker with target debt reduction indicators.

## Enhancing Approval Probability with Co-Applicants and Stable Income Proof
If a single applicant's individual debt-to-income profile is constrained, adding an earning family member (spouse or parent) as a co-applicant can increase aggregate household income and lower the blended FOIR. Providing comprehensive digital bank statements demonstrating consistent salary credits or business cashflows without inward cheque bounces significantly improves automated underwriting scores upon reapplication.
